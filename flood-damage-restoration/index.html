Flood damage emergency: safety before cleanup

Flood water is an emergency from the first inch, and the 24/7 line is open at any hour for Bartow callers. Before you call, make sure everyone is out of the water. The National Weather Service warns that six inches of fast-moving water can knock an adult off their feet, and twelve inches can carry away a small car.

If water has reached outlets, the panel or a gas appliance, do not wade in. Call the utility or 911, wait for the all-clear, then call us for same-day extraction when crews are available. Do not drive through flooded roads to get to the house. Soaked flooring is covered on carpet water damage and standing water on water extraction.

Why flood water is different from a pipe leak

A broken supply line starts clean. River, storm drain and runoff water does not, because it picks up soil, fuel, fertilizer and sewage on the way in. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard treats floodwater as Category 3, so carpet, pad and soaked drywall are removed rather than dried in place.

That is why flood work often looks like demolition first. Porous materials that sat in contaminated water cannot be reliably cleaned, and keeping them risks odor and mold. Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned, treated and dried.

Flooding history that matters for Bartow property owners

East Bartow and the Peace River

Polk County Emergency Management has noted that East Bartow has a history of flooding from the Peace River, which begins at Lake Hancock. After Hurricane Ian in 2022 the river stayed high for weeks, so a flooded home there may not be dry for a long time even after the sky clears.

During Hurricane Debby in August 2024, the City of Bartow declared an emergency with the river at 7.6 feet, and in October 2024 Hurricane Milton pushed Lake Hancock to emergency levels. If your address is near the river or the lake, check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your parcel and ask the city about local flood history before you rebuild.

Cleanup after floodwater leaves

  1. 1

    Pump out

    Water is removed gradually so soil and foundations are not stressed by sudden drainage.

  2. 2

    Remove

    Wet carpet, pad, insulation and drywall that touched floodwater are taken out and bagged.

  3. 3

    Clean and treat

    Framing, subfloor and hard surfaces are cleaned and treated.

  4. 4

    Dry and verify

    Dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture meters read dry.

Sewage sometimes comes with floods, and that cleanup has its own page on sewage backup. A flooded ground floor with finished walls often needs the basement and below-grade approach as well. Wear boots, gloves and an N-95 respirator when handling anything the water touched. For a neighborhood with a documented river flood history, see East Bartow.

Flood insurance is separate from homeowners coverage

Check for flood coverage early

FEMA states that homeowners and renters policies do not typically cover flood damage, and that a National Flood Insurance Program policy generally takes effect 30 days after purchase. Flood coverage can also come from private insurers, and some lenders require it.

The Florida Department of Financial Services says initial claims should be reported within one year of the date of loss. Photograph everything before it is moved, keep receipts, and call your insurer before signing a repair contract.

FAQ

Common questions

Do you handle flood damage cleanup emergencies at night or on weekends in Bartow?

Yes. The 24/7 line takes flood calls at night and on weekends, and same-day service is offered when crews are available and the site is safe to enter.

Can floodwater be dried in place like a pipe leak?

Rarely. Floodwater is Category 3, so soaked carpet, pad, insulation and drywall are normally removed, and hard surfaces are cleaned and dried.

Does homeowners insurance cover flood?

Typically not. FEMA says flood damage needs a separate flood policy, and new National Flood Insurance Program policies generally take effect 30 days after purchase.

How do I check whether my property is in a flood zone?

Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center with your address, and ask the City of Bartow or Polk County building department about local flood history.

Is the water safe to touch?

No. Floodwater can carry sewage and chemicals, so wear boots, gloves and an N-95 respirator and wash after contact.
