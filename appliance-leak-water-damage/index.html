Appliance leak emergency: stop the supply, then call

An appliance leak is an emergency because the supply line keeps feeding it until someone closes the valve. The 24/7 line takes calls at night and on weekends, and same-day service is offered when crews are available, so call once the water is stopped.

Shut the small valve behind the washer, under the sink or behind the refrigerator. If you cannot reach it, close the main water valve for the house. Unplug the appliance only if the outlet and the floor are dry, and otherwise leave it and call an electrician or the utility. When the water has reached the wall, see wall and drywall water damage repair and how drying is checked in structural drying.

Where appliance leaks come from

Washing machine hoses

The hot and cold fill hoses stay under supply pressure all day, so a split or loose coupling pours water behind the machine.

Dishwasher connections

A failed door gasket, supply line or drain hose leaks onto the kitchen floor and into the cabinet base.

Refrigerator ice-maker lines

A small line behind the fridge can drip for weeks and ruin the subfloor before anyone sees it.

Overflow and drain backups

A clogged washer standpipe or drain hose overflows, and that water can be gray rather than clean.

What to do before the crew arrives

  • Close the supply valve behind the appliance, or the main valve if you cannot reach it.
  • Unplug the appliance only if the outlet and the floor in front of it are dry.
  • Keep a household vacuum away from standing water, since it is an electrical risk.
  • Lift rugs, boxes and small electronics off the wet floor.
  • Photograph the appliance, the hose and the damage before cleanup.

Why water behind an appliance is easy to miss

A fridge or washer hides the wall and the floor behind it, so a leak can run for days with nothing but a faint musty smell. Soft flooring near the appliance, swollen baseboards or a stain on the ceiling below are all warning signs.

Crews pull the unit out when it is safe, then check the subfloor, baseboards and the back of cabinet bases with a meter. Wet cabinet kickboards are common in kitchens, and that case has its own page on kitchen water damage restoration.

Cleaning it up

Washing machine and dishwasher discharge can be gray water, which the S500 standard calls Category 2, so carpet pad and porous materials that absorbed it are usually removed. A supply-line leak is Category 1 at first, but it can slide to Category 2 if it sits.

  1. 1

    Extract

    Standing water is removed from the floor and the cabinet base.

  2. 2

    Inspect

    Moisture meters and a thermal camera check the subfloor, walls and ceiling below.

  3. 3

    Remove

    Wet pad, baseboard and swollen material is cut out where it cannot dry.

  4. 4

    Dry

    Air movers and a dehumidifier run until readings come back to normal.

Hardwood floors and laminate that have swollen need their own attention, covered on hardwood floor water damage repair. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold.

Insurance and the appliance itself

A sudden hose or line failure is often covered under the water damage portion of a homeowners policy, while the appliance itself and slow leaks that were ignored often are not. If you rent, tell your landlord right away and keep your own renters policy information handy.

Photograph the leak, the failed part and the damage before anything is moved, and keep the broken hose or line. Your insurer may ask for it, and a plumber or appliance technician can tell you whether the fitting failed from age or from installation.

FAQ

Common questions

Do you handle appliance leak water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Bartow?

Yes. The line is open 24/7, and same-day service is offered when crews are available.

Where is the shut-off for a washer?

Behind the machine there are two small valves, one for hot and one for cold. Turning both handles stops the fill hoses, and the main house valve is the backup.

Is washing machine water clean?

Not always. Fill water is clean, but drain and overflow water is gray and treated as Category 2.

Can the leak damage the floor below?

Yes. On an upper floor, water can pass through the subfloor and show up as a ceiling stain.

Will you fix the appliance?

No. An appliance technician or plumber repairs the unit or line, and the restoration crew handles water removal and drying.
