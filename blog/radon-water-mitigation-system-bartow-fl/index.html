Radon Mitigation vs Water Mitigation Systems in Bartow, FL

A radon mitigation system and a water mitigation job are not the same thing. One deals with a gas in the soil, and the other deals with liquid water in a building. This guide sorts out which is which and where to go for each.

Two different problems with similar names

Radon is a radioactive gas that comes from the ground and can enter a building through small openings in the foundation or through building materials. You cannot see, smell or taste it. A radon mitigation system is a set of pipes and a fan that pull the gas from under the building and vent it outside.

Water mitigation is the work that follows a leak or flood. It removes standing water and dries the structure. Companies like ours do water mitigation. Radon mitigation is a different trade that Florida regulates separately, and we do not install radon systems.

What the Florida Department of Health says about radon

The Florida Department of Health reports that about 1 in 5 Florida homes has radon above the EPA action level. The only way to know your level is to test. The department says the World Health Organization, the National Academy of Sciences, the US Department of Health and Human Services and the EPA all classify radon as a known human carcinogen.

If a test comes back high, the department says the problem can be fixed by installing a mitigation system. Each home needs a system designed for that building, and the design depends on the foundation type.

Who can install a radon system in Florida

All radon mitigation in Florida must be carried out by professionals certified by the Florida Department of Health. The department issues separate certificates for measurement and for mitigation, and an installation must be supervised by a certified mitigation specialist.

The department lists the items a mitigation business must provide. These include system labels, a visual or audible device that shows the system has failed, and vents above the eave of the roof for active systems. They also include written operating instructions and post-mitigation testing by a certified measurement business. It recommends reviewing these with the contractor before you sign.

How a radon system is checked after installation

  1. A warning device alerts you if the fan or other mechanical parts of an active system stop working.
  2. The system is labeled as a radon mitigation system, with the name of the certified business that installed it.
  3. A post-mitigation test is done within 30 days of installation, and not sooner than 24 hours after the system is running with the fan on.
  4. The warning device does not show whether radon rises, only that the mechanical parts failed, so retesting is still the way to confirm levels.

Where water and radon do overlap

The overlap is in how a building handles moisture and air. A sump, a sealed crawl space or a vapor barrier can affect both water problems and soil gas pathways. If you are planning work on a crawl space or slab, it makes sense to ask whether the contractors involved have thought about both.

Water that enters a building is a separate matter from radon in the air. A wet slab or crawl space is covered on crawl space water damage and slab leak water damage. Neither page is a substitute for a radon test.

Testing in Polk County

The county health department in Bartow

The Florida Department of Health in Polk County is based at 2090 E Clower St in Bartow, ZIP 33830. It offers radon test kits to residents. You can reach the office at 863-519-8330 to ask how to get one.

Test kits are also sold at hardware and department stores, and they can be ordered by mail. A certified measurement business can test for you if you prefer professional placement and reporting.

When you sell a home

Florida law requires a specific written disclosure about radon gas in connection with the sale of a building. The wording is a standard notice about the risk, and it applies whether or not you have tested. If you have test results or a mitigation system, keep the paperwork where you can find it.

This is also a reason to document any water loss and drying work. Buyers ask about both, and sellers who can produce records are in a better position. Your real estate agent or attorney can tell you what to attach.

Reading a radon test result

Radon is measured in picocuries per liter of air. The EPA action level is 4 picocuries per liter, and the Florida Department of Health uses that level when it says about 1 in 5 Florida homes is above it. A short-term test gives a quick reading, and a long-term test shows how levels change over the seasons.

If the first result is high, many professionals recommend a second test before spending money on a system. Whatever you decide, keep the report. It will matter when you sell, and it will help the certified specialist design the system for your foundation.

Misunderstandings worth clearing up

A wet basement means radon

Water in a building and radon in the air are separate problems. One does not prove the other.

A new home is automatically safe

The only way to know is to test, whatever the age of the building.

Water treatment removes radon from the air

Radon in the air is addressed by a mitigation system under the building, not by a filter on the plumbing.

Any contractor can fit a system

Florida requires certification from the Department of Health for radon mitigation.

Questions to ask a radon professional

  1. Are you certified by the Florida Department of Health for radon mitigation, and can I see the certificate?
  2. What system do you propose for my foundation, and why?
  3. Where will the pipe and fan go, and where will it vent?
  4. What warning device is included, and what does it tell me?
  5. Who does the post-mitigation test, and when?
  6. What maintenance will the system need over time?

The answers should be specific to your building. The department notes that each home requires a unique system designed for that building, so a quote that does not mention your foundation type is a reason to ask more.

Radon testing and moisture work on the same project

If you are repairing water damage and also testing for radon, schedule them with care. Open windows and large fans change the air and can affect a short-term test. Ask the person placing the test what conditions they need, and tell the drying crew when the test will run.

It also helps to keep each type of record separate. A water loss file holds photos, readings and invoices. A radon file holds test reports and system documents. Both are useful at a sale, and buyers read them differently.

What to do next

If your concern is radon, order a test and call a Florida certified professional if the result is high. If your concern is water in the building, call (844) 950-6900 and describe what happened. If it is both, the order is simple: dry the building first, because the radon test will be easier to read in a stable environment.
