Mold Removal Services: What to Expect From Start to Finish in Bartow

A mold job is more than scrubbing. In Florida it has stages, licensed roles and a written scope. This guide walks through what each stage covers so a quote makes sense when you see it.

By the Bartow Water Damage Pros team

The stages of a mold job

  1. 1

    Assessment

    A licensed mold assessor inspects, finds the moisture source and writes a protocol.

  2. 2

    Containment

    The work area is sealed with plastic, and air is filtered so spores stay put.

  3. 3

    Removal

    Mold-damaged porous materials are taken out, and hard surfaces are cleaned.

  4. 4

    Drying

    Equipment dries the structure so mold does not return.

  5. 5

    Clearance

    The assessor confirms the area meets the protocol.

The specifics of each stage depend on how much mold there is, what it is growing on and how the building is laid out. A bathroom wall and a whole attic are not the same job.

Assessment is the first and separate service

A Florida mold assessor is licensed by the Department of Business and Professional Regulation. The assessor looks for visible growth, checks for moisture, may take samples and writes a protocol that says what to remove and how. The protocol is the document a remediator follows.

Because an assessor may not remediate a structure they assessed within the previous 12 months, the assessment and the cleanup come from different companies. That is a feature of the system. It puts a second set of eyes on the work.

What remediation includes

  • Setting up containment barriers and, where the protocol calls for it, negative air machines.
  • Removing mold-damaged drywall, insulation, carpet or ceiling material and bagging it for disposal.
  • Cleaning hard surfaces such as framing with HEPA vacuuming and damp wiping.
  • Treating and drying the area so the moisture does not return.
  • Cleaning the surrounding rooms to remove dust created by the work.

The industry reference is the IICRC S520 standard. It sets out general practices for controlling and removing mold, and a contractor who works to it can show you how.

What is usually not in the quote

Fixing the leak

A plumber or roofer repairs the source. Remediation deals with the mold that resulted from it.

The rebuild

New drywall, paint, trim and flooring are normally a separate scope after clearance.

Assessment and testing

In Florida this is a separate license and usually a separate invoice.

Contents

Furniture, clothes and belongings may need cleaning or replacement that is quoted separately.

Ask for the scope in writing and ask what each line item covers. A clear quote lets you compare two companies on the same basis rather than the same bottom line.

Insurance and mold

Mold coverage varies by policy. Many homeowners policies limit or exclude mold, and some cover it only when it follows a covered water loss. Contact your insurer before you sign a contract, as the Florida Department of Financial Services advises, and ask what documents it wants from the assessor and the remediator.

If the mold follows a water loss that is covered, the quickest way to keep the claim clean is to document the loss, the drying and the mold work in order. Photos and moisture readings taken early help show the mold was a result of the leak and not an older condition.

How long the process takes

A small contained job can be done in a day or two, while a large attic or a whole-house job takes longer. The assessment, the protocol and the clearance visit add days on either side of the cleanup. Scheduling often depends on availability for testing and lab results.

That is one reason to start with the moisture. If the leak is still active, no schedule will hold, because the area has to be dry before the job is considered done.

Preparing your home for the crew

  • Clear the area around the work zone so the crew can set up barriers.
  • Remove valuables and small items from rooms next to the work area.
  • Ask whether the HVAC system should be off, and follow the instructions.
  • Plan for noise from air filtration machines, which run through the job.
  • Ask where you and your pets should stay if the work area is close to living space.

The protocol often covers these points, and the remediator should walk you through them before the first day. Ask about them at the quote stage so you can plan for access and noise.

After the job: keeping it dry

A clean result does not stay clean unless the moisture is controlled. Keep indoor humidity below 60 percent, as the EPA advises, and ideally between 30 and 50 percent. Fix any leak found along the way, clean the AC condensate drain and run bathroom fans to the outside.

Check the area every few weeks for the first season. A musty smell, a returning stain or a soft spot is a reason to call the assessor back. A return of the problem usually means the water source was not fully solved.

A note for Bartow homeowners

Who to check with in the city and county

The City of Bartow Building Department at 450 N Wilson Ave issues building, electrical, plumbing and mechanical permits. Polk County Building at 330 W Church St serves unincorporated areas. If a mold job involves rebuilding walls or replacing electrical or plumbing, ask which office applies to your address.

Permits are separate from the mold work itself, but they often come up when the structure is opened and repairs begin.
