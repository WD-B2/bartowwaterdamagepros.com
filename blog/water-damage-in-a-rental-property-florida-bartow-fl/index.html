Water Damage in a Rental Property: Who Does What in Bartow, FL

When water gets into a rental, tenants want it fixed and landlords want to know their duties. This guide explains the Florida process in plain language, without taking sides.

By the Bartow Water Damage Pros team

24/7 emergency line(844) 950-6900Call now

The landlord duty in one sentence

Florida Statute 83.51 requires a residential landlord to comply with applicable building, housing and health codes and to maintain the roof, windows, floors, steps, porches, exterior walls, foundations and plumbing in good condition. Water damage often sits at the edge of those duties, which is why records matter.

This is general information and not legal advice. Leases differ, and some facts change the answer, such as who caused the leak. Both sides can ask a Florida landlord-tenant attorney or a local legal aid office about their situation.

What a tenant does first

  1. 1

    Stop the water if safe

    Close the fixture valve or the main valve if you know where it is.

  2. 2

    Tell the landlord now

    Call or text so there is a record, and follow up in writing.

  3. 3

    Document

    Take dated photos and video of the source, the water and the damaged belongings.

  4. 4

    Protect belongings

    Move items out of wet areas, and keep receipts for what you spend.

  5. 5

    Check your own insurance

    Renters insurance may cover belongings, and the landlord policy generally covers the building.

If the water is spreading and the landlord cannot be reached, calling a restoration company to limit the damage is reasonable. Tell the landlord what you did and why.

The seven day written notice

Florida law gives a landlord seven days after receiving written notice to correct a failure to maintain the premises. If the landlord does not comply, the tenant may have the right to withhold rent or to end the lease, if the notice was done correctly. The notice has to be in writing and delivered the way the law and the lease allow, and extra days are added when it is mailed.

Any withheld rent has to be handled carefully. If a landlord files for eviction, a tenant who withheld rent generally has to pay it into the court registry. This is the sort of step where a legal aid office or attorney is worth a call before you act.

What a landlord should do

  • Respond quickly and keep a record of when you were told.
  • Stop the water and bring in a restoration company, because delay raises costs and the risk of mold.
  • Notify your own insurer promptly.
  • Give the tenant a clear timeline and tell them if they need to leave temporarily.
  • Keep invoices, readings and photos in a single folder.

A landlord who acts at once is in a stronger position than one who waits for the written notice. If the damage is severe, see how the work runs on burst pipe water damage or appliance leak water damage.

Whose insurance pays for what

Building and fixtures

The landlord policy normally responds to the structure, subject to its terms.

Tenant belongings

A renters policy covers personal property, and without one the tenant bears the loss unless the landlord is at fault.

Extra living costs

Some renters policies cover temporary housing when a unit cannot be used.

Tenant-caused damage

If the tenant caused the leak, the landlord may look to the tenant or the tenant policy.

Mold in a rental

Mold follows water that stays. If a tenant sees mold, it usually means a leak or a humidity problem was not fixed. The EPA says to control moisture, and for large areas, over about 10 square feet, to consider a professional. Mold work in Florida is performed by licensed mold remediators, as regulated by the Department of Business and Professional Regulation.

Tenants should not clean up large mold on their own, and should send photos to the landlord. Landlords should not delay, since a dry unit is cheaper to fix than a moldy one. For the service itself, see mold remediation.

Common rental water problems around Bartow

Aging buildings and rainy season

Bartow has residential districts that date from the 1880s to the early 1900s, and many rentals sit in older buildings with original plumbing and roofs. The National Weather Service Tampa Bay office defines the rainy season as May 25 to October 10, which is when roof and window leaks tend to show up.

Tenants and landlords both benefit from a quick look at ceilings and under sinks before the wet season starts.

Water heaters, supply lines and AC drain pans cause many rental leaks. See AC leak water damage and water heater leak cleanup. Nearby towns with rentals are covered on the Highland City and Mulberry pages. For emergency dry-out, call (844) 950-6900.

If the unit is not livable

Sometimes a unit cannot be used for a time. Florida law has provisions for a tenant to end the lease when a unit is rendered wholly untenantable, and for rent to abate until repairs are made after a storm. These details depend on the facts and on the lease, so get advice before relying on them.

In the meantime, keep all receipts for hotels and extra costs. Some renters policies cover temporary housing, and the receipts are the evidence. Send a written message to the landlord with the date you left and the reason.

What a written notice should include

  • The date and your address.
  • A clear description of the water damage and its cause, if known.
  • A statement that this is a request to repair under the lease and Florida law.
  • Photos attached, with the dates they were taken.
  • How you delivered it, and a copy kept for yourself.

Keep the language factual. Do not threaten, and do not guess at legal remedies in the notice. If you want to withhold rent or end the lease, get advice about the precise wording and timing first.

A short note on deposits and move-out

If a unit is damaged by water and the tenant leaves, the way the deposit is handled depends on who caused the damage and what the lease says. Photos from move-in and photos of the damage help both sides. Written messages that show when the tenant reported the problem protect a tenant from being blamed for damage that grew while waiting for a repair.

Landlords should keep invoices and readings in case a deposit is disputed. Both sides benefit from being clear about dates.

Keep records either way

Dated photos, written messages, invoices and moisture readings protect both sides. A landlord can show that they acted. A tenant can show what they reported and when. If the dispute goes further, the person with the better records usually has the better position.

It also helps to keep a copy of the lease and any renters policy in the same folder. If the unit is not livable for a time, the lease and policy often decide who pays for what. Drying work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.
