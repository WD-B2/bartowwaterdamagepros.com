Musty Smell in Your House After a Leak: Where to Look in Bartow, FL

A musty smell usually means something is damp, even when nothing looks wet. This guide lists the common hiding places in Florida homes and the checks that tell you what is going on.

By the Bartow Water Damage Pros team

24/7 emergency line(844) 950-6900Call now

What the smell is telling you

A musty odor is the smell of damp materials. It can come from mold, from decaying wet wood or paper, or from bacteria in standing water. The smell alone does not tell you which. What it does tell you is that moisture is present, and moisture is the thing to find.

The EPA advises controlling moisture and keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50 percent. If a room smells musty and a hygrometer reads high, that is a lead worth following even before you see a stain.

Check the easy places first

  • Under sinks, where supply lines and drain traps leak slowly.
  • Behind and under the washer, dishwasher and refrigerator.
  • Around the base of toilets and tubs.
  • Inside closets on exterior walls.
  • Around windows and doors after rain.

Press on baseboards and the bottom of drywall. Soft or cool material suggests moisture. A flashlight at a low angle can show swelling or ripples that disappear in normal light.

The air conditioning system

In Florida, the AC works more than half the year, and it makes a lot of water. A clogged condensate line or a full drain pan can spill into the ceiling, the closet or the air handler cabinet. A musty smell when the system starts is a common sign.

The EPA advises not running the HVAC system if you suspect mold, since it can spread spores through the house. If the odor comes from the vents, turn the system off and have it inspected. The water side is covered on AC leak water damage.

Crawl spaces, slabs and walls

Crawl space

Wet soil and open vents let moisture in. The smell travels up through the floor. See crawl space water damage.

Slab

A leak under the slab shows up as warm spots, damp flooring or a smell, and the water bill may rise. See slab leak water damage.

Wall cavity

A pinhole leak inside a wall wets insulation and drywall behind a clean surface.

Attic

A roof leak wets insulation and decking, and the smell comes down through the ceiling.

How a moisture check works

A moisture inspection uses a meter to read how wet materials are and a thermal camera to see cool, damp areas behind surfaces. It can find a wet wall without opening it, and it gives you readings you can compare after drying. That is much more useful than guessing from a smell.

If the readings are high, the next step is to find the source and dry the area. If the readings are normal and the smell persists, the cause may be dust, a dirty drain or old odors trapped in fabric, and cleaning can solve it. Professional drying follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which uses readings like these.

What to do while you wait

  1. 1

    Open windows when humidity allows

    Fresh air helps, but not during a rainy afternoon when humidity is high.

  2. 2

    Run a dehumidifier

    It lowers the moisture in the air that feeds the smell.

  3. 3

    Do not mask it

    Air fresheners and sprays hide the clue you need.

  4. 4

    Take notes

    Write down when you smell it, where and what the weather was like.

Bartow conditions

Humid months and hidden water

The National Weather Service Tampa Bay office defines the West Central Florida rainy season as May 25 to October 10, and hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30. Humidity stays high for much of that time, which means any damp material dries slowly.

A musty smell that shows up in late summer is often the delayed result of a leak or high humidity from earlier in the season.

A short checklist for the next week

  1. Buy or borrow a hygrometer and check the rooms that smell.
  2. Check under sinks and around appliances every day for a week.
  3. Look at the AC drain line and pan.
  4. Photograph any stain, and mark its edge with a pencil to see if it grows.
  5. Call for a moisture check if readings stay high or the stain grows.

Different smells, different clues

Earthy and damp

This usually points to moisture in wood, drywall or carpet.

Sour or stale

Often a dry or dirty drain trap, or a damp fabric or sponge-like material.

Sewage

A strong sewer smell points to a drain, trap or sewer line problem and should be handled quickly.

Gas

If you smell natural gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not look for the source yourself.

Smell is a clue and not a measurement. A moisture meter and a thermal camera give you numbers and pictures. If the smell goes away when the humidity drops, you have learned something. If it comes back with every rain, look at the roof and the walls.

Keeping a moisture diary

A simple diary helps you and anyone who inspects the house later. Each day for a week, note the date, the room, the weather, the humidity reading and whether you smell anything. Add a photo of any stain with a coin or a ruler beside it for scale.

Patterns show up fast. A smell that rises on rainy afternoons points to the roof or the walls. A smell that rises when the AC runs points to the system. A smell that stays constant points to something stored in a damp area, like a soaked box or carpet.

When the smell is a sign of something bigger

A smell of sewage is different from a musty odor. It points to a drain, a dried trap or a sewer line problem, and it should be addressed quickly. Sewage is contaminated, and the EPA says it needs a professional. See sewage backup cleanup for what that involves.

If you see visible mold larger than about 10 square feet, the EPA says to consider a professional. Mold work in Florida is licensed through the Department of Business and Professional Regulation, as covered on mold remediation.
