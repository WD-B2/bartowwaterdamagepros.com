Storm Season Water Damage Prep for Bartow, FL Homes

Most storm preparation is boring and cheap compared with the cleanup that follows. This checklist is for the weeks before a storm, when you still have time.

By the Bartow Water Damage Pros team

24/7 emergency line(844) 950-6900Call now

Know the calendar

The National Weather Service Tampa Bay office defines the West Central Florida rainy season as May 25 to October 10. Hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30. The overlap, from June through early October, is when water damage calls spike.

You do not need to prepare for every afternoon storm. You do need a plan for the big ones, and the plan works best when you make it before the forecast shows a cone over Polk County.

Roof and exterior

  • Look for missing, lifted or cracked shingles or tiles from the ground, or hire someone to look.
  • Check flashing around chimneys, vents and skylights.
  • Clear gutters and downspouts so water leaves the roof edge.
  • Trim branches that hang over the roof.
  • Seal gaps around windows, doors and wall penetrations.

A stain on a ceiling after rain often means a roof or flashing problem. Ceilings that have been wet are covered on ceiling water damage repair, and wind-driven rain is on storm water damage cleanup.

Drainage around the house

Water that runs toward a house is one of the most common reasons for ground-level leaks. The soil around the foundation should slope away. Downspouts should discharge several feet from the wall. Low spots where water pools should be filled or drained.

If you have a crawl space, check that vents and the ground cover are intact. Water standing under the house is a mold risk later. See crawl space water damage.

Know your main valve and breaker panel

  1. 1

    Find the main water valve

    Make sure everyone in the house can find it and turn it.

  2. 2

    Label the breaker panel

    Know which breakers feed the rooms most at risk.

  3. 3

    Test the sump or drain

    If you have one, check it runs before the rain.

  4. 4

    Move valuables up

    Store documents and electronics above floor level in low-lying rooms.

Insurance and the 30 day rule

FEMA notes that homeowners policies do not typically cover flood and that a National Flood Insurance Program policy generally takes effect 30 days after purchase. If you plan to buy flood coverage, do it well before a storm. The FEMA Flood Map Service Center shows the zone for your address.

Review your homeowners policy for hurricane and wind deductibles and for any water backup coverage. Take a video walk-through of each room now, and keep it somewhere safe. Photographs of the house before damage are a useful comparison for a claim.

Understand the flood risk of water that moves

The National Weather Service says six inches of moving water can knock over an adult and a foot of water can carry a small car. Do not walk or drive through floodwater. Floodwater is also contaminated, so it is treated differently from a clean pipe leak.

River and surface flood cleanup is covered on flood damage restoration. Plan to stay out of the house if it has flooded until power and structure have been checked.

What Bartow storms have done recently

Ian, Debby and Milton

After Hurricane Ian in 2022, East Bartow flooded from the Peace River and the water stayed high for weeks. In August 2024, Tropical Storm Debby brought the river to 7.6 feet and prompted an emergency declaration. In October 2024, Hurricane Milton pushed Lake Hancock to emergency levels.

Three storms in three calendar years is a reason to treat the checklist as routine. The pattern in each case was slow-rising water, not just rain through a roof.

A go bag for paperwork

Put the insurance policy, the agent phone number, photos of the house and a list of valuables in a waterproof bag or a cloud folder. A copy of the deed and mortgage information is also helpful. If you have to leave, you can take it with you or reach it from a phone.

Add the number of the restoration company you plan to call so you do not have to search for it in the middle of a storm.

Preparing the inside of the house

  • Move electronics and papers off the floor in ground-level rooms.
  • Unplug small appliances that sit low.
  • Know where the main water valve and breaker panel are.
  • Photograph or video each room, including closets.
  • Fill your phone with the numbers you will need: insurer, agent, utilities and a restoration line.

None of this stops a storm, but all of it reduces the damage and the time you spend looking for things afterward.

A small supply list

A flashlight, batteries, a tarp, plastic sheeting, tape, a bucket and work gloves cover the basics. A portable phone charger and a printed list of contacts are worth having when power is out. Keep them in one place that you can reach in the dark.

If the storm brings water into the house, these supplies help you protect belongings until a crew arrives. They do not replace professional drying, which is still the main way to prevent mold in the days after.

Different houses, different weak points

Older houses often have original roofs, single-pane windows and plaster that takes water up slowly. Newer houses often have tighter construction that traps moisture when it gets in. Slab homes lose less to flooding from below than raised homes, but a slab leak is harder to find. Houses with attached garages have a door and a threshold that rain finds first.

Walk your property with those weak points in mind. Look at the roof edge, the window sills, the garage door seal and the low spots in the yard, and fix what you can before the next storm.

After the storm

Wait for the all-clear before you go back into a flooded area. Photograph the damage before temporary repairs, as the Florida Department of Financial Services advises, and contact your insurer before signing repair contracts. Dry wet materials as soon as it is safe, because the EPA recommends drying within 24 to 48 hours.

Everyone around you will need help at once, so call early. A short call that describes the loss and location is the fastest way to be in line. Professional drying follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.
