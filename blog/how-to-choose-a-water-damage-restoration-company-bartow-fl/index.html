How to Choose a Water Damage Restoration Company in Bartow, FL

When water is in your house, you will probably call the first company that answers. This guide gives you a short checklist for that call so you can tell a careful company from a rushed one.

By the Bartow Water Damage Pros team

What a good first call sounds like

A careful dispatcher asks questions before promising anything. Where did the water come from, is it still running, how deep is it and what rooms are affected. The questions tell the crew what to bring and give you a first read on whether the company understands water.

Be wary of a call that jumps straight to a price or an arrival time. Scope and price are explained before work starts, and neither can be settled well without information about the loss.

Ask about standards

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard covers professional water damage restoration, and the S520 standard covers mold remediation. Ask whether the company works to them and how. A good answer mentions water categories, moisture readings and drying goals.

A standard is not a badge you can print on a van. It is a method. You can judge it by whether the company can explain what it will do and why.

Ask what you will get in writing

  • A written scope that lists what will be removed, cleaned and dried.
  • A written price, or a clear explanation of what will determine it.
  • Moisture readings at the start and during drying.
  • Photos of the damage and of the work.
  • A list of what is not included, such as the rebuild.

A company that is unwilling to put its scope on paper is telling you something. You are entitled to understand the job before signing.

Licensing and insurance

Ask whether the company is licensed and insured, and ask to see proof. Water mitigation itself is different from mold work. Mold assessment and mold remediation are licensed by the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation, and an assessor may not remediate a structure they assessed within the previous 12 months.

If your job involves plumbing, a water heater or structural repair, the person doing that work may need to be licensed and to pull a permit. Ask who is responsible. The company should be able to tell you.

Contracts to read slowly

Assignment of benefits

Florida has banned assignments of post-loss benefits under property policies issued or renewed on or after January 1, 2023. Do not sign a document that transfers your insurance rights.

Pay in full up front

Be cautious of a large payment before any work is done.

Open-ended scope

A contract that says anything needed, with no limits, deserves questions.

Pressure to sign now

The Department of Financial Services advises contacting your insurer before signing a repair contract.

Questions that reveal experience

  1. How do you decide what can be dried and what has to come out?
  2. How do you know when drying is finished?
  3. What do you do if readings stall?
  4. What documents do you give me for my insurer?
  5. Who will be on site, and who do I call if I have a question?

You do not need to be a technician to hear whether the answers are specific. Specific answers about readings, equipment and timing beat general reassurance.

What to avoid promising yourself

No honest company can quote an exact price from a phone description, and none can promise an exact drying time. Both depend on what is found at the property. What you can expect is a plain explanation of the scope, the factors that move the price and a plan for checking progress.

The same goes for insurance. A company can document the loss and explain the work, but your insurer decides coverage. If someone tells you what your policy will pay before you have spoken to the insurer, treat that as a guess.

Comparing two quotes

Two quotes with different totals are only comparable if the scopes match. One may include more equipment days, more removal or the cleaning of contents, and the other may leave those out. Put the line items side by side and ask each company to explain the differences.

Do not pick on price alone. A lower figure that leaves out drying checks can cost more when the walls stay wet and mold follows. A higher figure that explains each step can be the better value.

Signs of a careful crew on site

They measure

Moisture meters and a thermal camera come out before equipment is set.

They explain

The crew tells you what is coming out and what is staying, and why.

They protect

Floors, furniture and unaffected rooms are protected from foot traffic and dust.

They return

Equipment is checked each day, and readings are written down.

A crew that does not measure, does not explain and does not return is working from habit. You are allowed to ask what the readings are and what the target is.

If you have already signed something

Ask the company for a copy of everything you signed. Read it with your insurer on the phone if you can. The Florida Department of Financial Services advises contacting your insurer before signing a repair contract, so it is worth doing that after the fact if you did not before.

If a document transfers your insurance rights, call your insurer right away and ask what it means for your claim. Florida bans assignment of benefits for property policies issued or renewed on or after January 1, 2023.

Speed versus care

Fast matters in water damage, because the longer materials stay wet, the more of them have to come out. But fast is not the same as careless. A crew that arrives quickly and starts pulling out walls without readings may remove more than necessary, and a crew that dawdles may let the damage spread.

The best sign is a crew that moves quickly on the things that cannot wait, such as stopping the water and extracting standing water, and slows down for the decisions that depend on measurement. Ask which is which, and you will learn a lot about how the company thinks.

Local knowledge helps

Why Bartow experience is practical

A company that works around Bartow knows that the city runs its own water, sewer and electric utilities. It knows that permits come from the Building Department at 450 N Wilson Ave or from Polk County Building at 330 W Church St. And it knows that East Bartow has a history of Peace River flooding.

That context shapes the job. A flood is not a plumbing leak, and an unincorporated address has different permit and utility contacts from a city one.
