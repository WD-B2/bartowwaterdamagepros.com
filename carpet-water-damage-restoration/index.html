Carpet is soaking: what decides whether it can be saved

Two things decide whether wet carpet stays or goes: the kind of water and how long it sat. Clean water caught within a day or two can usually be extracted and dried in place. Contaminated water or water that has sat for days usually means the carpet and pad are removed.

The EPA says water-damaged carpet and backing should be dried within 24 to 48 hours, using water extraction, dehumidifiers and fans. That window is the practical line between a save and a replacement.

Carpet, pad and subfloor are three separate problems

The carpet

Synthetic carpet fibers do not absorb much water, so the carpet itself can often be dried and reinstalled.

The pad

Foam or rebond pad acts like a sponge and holds water, so it is usually replaced even when the carpet is saved.

The subfloor

Wood or concrete beneath the pad must be dried, or it will keep feeding moisture back into the room.

The tack strip and baseboard

Perimeter wood and trim wick water and are checked with a meter.

What to do first with wet carpet, and how we judge it

  • Lift furniture off the carpet and put foil or blocks under the legs.
  • Blot standing water with towels and do not use a household vacuum.
  • Open the room to airflow and run the air conditioner, which pulls moisture from the air.
  • Photograph the carpet and the stain before anything is moved.

Under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration, carpet that has been wet with contaminated water is generally removed, while carpet wet with clean water can often be dried in place if the pad dries too. Readings, not appearance, decide which applies.

Typical carpet water damage scenarios

A washing machine hose bursts and soaks the hall carpet. A water heater tank fails in a closet and spreads into bedroom carpet. A toilet or tub overflows upstairs and drips through to the carpet below.

Clean-water events like the first two are the best candidates for saving carpet. An overflow from a toilet or a sewer backup puts contaminated water into the carpet, and then it is removed. See sewage backup cleanup for those cases.

How wet carpet is handled

  1. 1

    Extract

    Weighted extraction wands pull water out of the carpet and pad.

  2. 2

    Lift and inspect

    The carpet is pulled back so the pad and subfloor can be checked.

  3. 3

    Dry

    Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the carpet, and replaced pad is installed once the floor is dry.

  4. 4

    Sanitize

    If the water was gray, carpet is cleaned and treated, or removed if it cannot be.

The standing water step is on water extraction. If the carpet is on a concrete slab and has been wet for a long time, the cause may be underneath, so a plumber may check for a slab leak.

Odor and mold concerns after wet carpet

A musty smell after a carpet leak usually means moisture is still in the pad or subfloor. Drying and replacing the pad usually clears it. Persistent smells or visible growth call for testing and remediation, which is covered on mold remediation.

The EPA recommends keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50 percent, and drying water-damaged items within 24 to 48 hours. Wear an N-95 respirator if you handle visible mold.

FAQ

Common questions

Can wet carpet be dried in place?

Often, if the water was clean and the carpet was extracted and dried within about 24 to 48 hours. The pad is usually replaced.

Why is the pad replaced?

Pad soaks up water like a sponge and is difficult to dry, and it can hold odor and bacteria. Carpet can often be saved and put back on new pad.

What if the water was dirty?

Gray or black water means the carpet and pad are removed. The EPA advises a professional for contaminated water.

Will the carpet shrink?

Some carpets can shrink or lose their backing bond, and a technician can check whether it can be restretched.

Is wet carpet a mold risk?

Yes if it stays wet. Drying quickly is the best protection.
