Hardwood Floor Water Damage Repair in Bartow, FL

What water does to a wood floor

Wood swells when it takes on water, and it swells unevenly because the underside of a board absorbs first. That is why boards edge up into a cup, with the sides higher than the middle, or lift off the subfloor in a buckle.

A floor that looks fine the day after a leak may start to cup a week later, as moisture works up through the board. For that reason moisture readings, not appearance, decide whether a floor can be saved.

Cupping, crowning and buckling

Cupping and crowning

  • Board edges higher than the center
  • Often caused by moisture from below
  • Can flatten after thorough drying
  • May need sanding once stable

Buckling

  • Boards lift off the subfloor
  • Usually from heavy or prolonged water
  • Often means the floor has to be removed
  • Check the subfloor too

Crowning is the reverse of cupping, with the center higher. It can appear after a cupped floor is sanded too soon, which is why refinishing waits until moisture is normal. Pet stains and dark rings on stained floors are surface damage and are handled differently from structural swelling.

What to do before we arrive: wet hardwood

  • Mop up standing water and lift rugs and furniture off the boards.
  • Do not sand, seal or refinish while the wood is wet.
  • Avoid walking on boards that are cupping or lifting.
  • Photograph the floor now, because cupping can change quickly.

Typical hardwood water damage scenarios

A dishwasher or refrigerator line leaks for days and edges of the floor cup near the kitchen. A slab leak or plumbing line below pushes moisture up through the floor from underneath. A window left open in a storm lets rain run across a plank floor.

The first scenario is covered on appliance leak water damage, and the second on slab leak water damage. Each one ends in the same question: can the floor be dried in place, or does it have to come up?

How hardwood is dried

  1. 1

    Measure

    A meter checks boards, the subfloor and unaffected reference areas.

  2. 2

    Extract

    Surface water is removed with weighted extractors and wet vacuums.

  3. 3

    Dry in place

    Air movers, dehumidifiers and drying mats pull moisture out of the wood.

  4. 4

    Decide

    Once readings stabilize, the floor is flattened, sanded, refinished or replaced.

Hardwood is a slow-drying material, which the S500 standard places in the specialty drying class. Drying can take longer than for drywall, so the schedule is built around readings. The equipment is described on structural drying and dehumidification. Older wood floors in the historic residential streets are discussed on South Bartow.

When to refinish and when to replace

Refinishing makes sense when boards have returned to a stable moisture level and are flat enough to sand. Replacement is more likely when boards are buckled, when mold has grown on the underside, or when the subfloor is also damaged.

Solid wood, engineered wood and laminate behave differently. Laminate and thin engineered flooring swell at the seams and often cannot be dried back to flat, so ask what your floor is made of before deciding.

FAQ

Common questions

Can a cupped hardwood floor be saved?

Often, if it is dried quickly and thoroughly. Cupping can flatten once moisture returns to normal, and the floor may then be sanded and refinished.

How long should I wait before refinishing?

Until moisture readings are stable, which can take weeks for thick wood floors. Sanding too soon can cause crowning later.

Will laminate recover from a leak?

Rarely. Laminate and thin engineered boards swell at the seams and often have to be replaced.

Why is the floor getting worse after the leak was fixed?

Moisture keeps working through the wood and subfloor for days. Drying with meters and equipment is the way to stop it.

Does insurance cover refinishing?

It may cover the damage from a sudden leak, and policies differ on matching. Ask your insurer.
