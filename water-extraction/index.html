When standing water is an emergency

Water that is still on the floor is an emergency in Bartow whether it arrived at noon or at 3 a.m. The line is open 24/7, and same-day service is offered when crews are available, so call as soon as you see water that will not drain on its own.

Until a crew arrives, shut off the main water valve if a pipe is the source, and turn off power to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water. If water touches outlets, a panel or a gas appliance, stay out and call the utility or 911 first, then call us. When the water came from a split line, see burst pipe water damage, and for soaked flooring see carpet water damage.

While you wait for the crew

  • Move rugs, shoes, papers and electronics to a dry surface above the waterline.
  • Photograph the water, the source and any damaged items before anything is moved.
  • Keep children and pets out of the water, especially if it came from a drain or toilet.
  • Do not run a household vacuum on water, and leave ceiling fixtures that hold water alone.

What water extraction actually removes

Extraction is bulk removal. Submersible pumps move deep water out through hose lines, and wet-dry extractors and weighted carpet wands pull the layer that soaks into carpet, pad and flooring. A single quarter inch of water over a 1,000 square foot floor is about 156 gallons, which weighs more than 1,300 pounds.

That number is why a household shop vacuum is not enough. Water that stays in place keeps wicking into drywall and baseboards, so the first hour of pumping matters more than any later step.

Why pumping is only the first stage

Once the standing water is gone, the structure still holds moisture. Subfloors, wall cavities and the bottom of drywall stay wet for days unless air movers and dehumidifiers pull it out. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to keep mold from starting.

Crews read moisture meters on walls and floors before they leave, then set equipment based on those readings. The drying stage has its own page, structural drying and dehumidification, and most extraction jobs lead straight into it.

Water category changes the plan

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard sorts water into three categories, and the category decides what can be dried and what has to be removed. Ask any company whether its crews work to that standard before you sign anything.

CategoryTypical sourceWhat it changes
Category 1Supply line, tap or tank that was clean when it spilledMost materials can be extracted and dried in place if the water has not sat.
Category 2Dishwasher or washing machine discharge, aquarium, toilet overflow without fecesCarpet pad is usually removed, and affected surfaces are cleaned and dried.
Category 3Sewage, river or surface flood water, anything that has sat long enough to turnPorous materials come out, and the area is treated as contaminated. See sewage backup cleanup.

Clean water can slide to a higher category if it sits, which is another reason to call early.

Where water tends to collect in Bartow homes

Homes in the Northeast Bartow and South Bartow historic residential districts date from the 1880s to the early 1900s. Older plaster walls and wood subfloors take water up slowly, so a floor that looks dry after pumping can still be wet underneath and needs readings, not a visual check.

On the east side of the city, where the Peace River floodplain begins near Lake Hancock, rising surface water is a different problem from a failed pipe. That situation belongs on the flood damage restoration page, because river water is Category 3 from the start.

FAQ

Common questions

Do you handle water extraction emergencies at night or on weekends in Bartow?

Yes. The line is open 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Same-day service is offered when crews are available, and dispatch asks about depth, source and electrical risk so the right equipment goes out.

How is water extraction different from drying?

Extraction removes standing water and the water held in carpet and pad. Drying removes the moisture left in the framing, subfloor and drywall, and it usually runs for days after the pumps are packed up.

Can I use a wet vacuum myself?

For a small spill from a clean source, yes. For anything deeper than a thin film, or any water near electrical outlets, a home vacuum cannot keep up and can be unsafe.

Will the carpet have to be removed?

It depends on the water category and how long it sat. Clean water caught quickly can often be extracted and dried in place, while contaminated water means the carpet and pad come out.

Does extraction find hidden water?

Pumping alone does not. Moisture meters and thermal imaging after extraction show where water went into walls and under floors, and that is where the drying plan is built.
