What downtown Bartow is, and where its edges are

Downtown Bartow is the historic center of the Polk County seat. The National Register of Historic Places lists the Bartow Downtown Commercial District, an area of roughly seven acres bounded by Davidson and Summerlin Streets and by Broadway and Florida Avenues. The Old Polk County Courthouse, finished in 1909, anchors the area.

The mix here is shops, offices, civic buildings and some homes a few blocks off Main Street. People use the name more loosely than the listing does. When you call, give the street address and the nearest cross street. That tells us more than a district name does.

Older masonry, plaster and wood floors

Age does not make a building leak, but it changes how a leak is checked. The district listing describes Classical Revival, vernacular and masonry buildings, and the earliest date given for the district is 1885. Brick walls, plaster and older wood floors hold moisture differently from new drywall and carpet.

A drying plan starts by finding which materials are actually wet. Moisture meters and a thermal camera show water that moved behind a wall or above a ceiling, and the readings decide how long equipment stays. A failed supply line or a roof leak on an upper floor often shows up first as a stain on the ceiling below, which is covered on ceiling water damage repair.

For a shop or office, the first hour matters

Tell the building owner

In a leased space, the landlord may control the roof, the risers and the shut-offs. Call the owner or manager while you call us.

Protect stock and records

Move paper, electronics and inventory off the floor and away from ceiling drips when it is safe to touch them.

Keep customers out

Wet tile, wet carpet and a dripping ceiling are slip and shock hazards. Block off the area before anyone walks through.

Photograph before you clean

Take wide and close photos of the source, the wet surfaces and the contents. A tenant policy and a building policy are often separate.

A business that cannot open loses time as well as property. Storefront and office jobs are covered on commercial water damage restoration, and standing water is handled by water extraction.

Permits and review inside a historic district

The City of Bartow Building Department at 450 N Wilson Ave issues building, electrical, plumbing and mechanical permits. The city also has a Historic Architectural Review Board that reviews alterations, demolitions and relocations of contributing properties and new construction inside designated historic district boundaries. It meets on the first Thursday of the month when it has items to hear.

Emergency extraction and drying are different from rebuilding an exterior feature. Ask the city before you change the outside of an older building. The City Clerk can send agendas, and the Planning Department can say whether a repair needs review.

What downtown storms have and have not shown

A fallen oak, not a river

We found no source reporting river flooding of downtown buildings, so this page does not call downtown flood prone. The Peace River history that officials describe in Bartow is tied to East Bartow.

Wind is another route for water. During Hurricane Ian in 2022 a large oak fell across East Church Street beside the Polk County Property Appraiser's Office. Limbs and roof openings let rain into buildings. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified, so check an address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

From standing water to dry walls

Extraction comes first because water keeps soaking into floors and walls while it sits. Air movers and dehumidifiers follow. The EPA says wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold growth, and readings show when a room is truly dry. The method is explained on structural drying and dehumidification.

Insurance records for a downtown loss

Coverage depends on what caused the damage and on the policy terms. Ask your insurer what it needs, and report the loss promptly. The Florida Department of Financial Services says a claim should be reported within one year of the loss.

Dated photos, a written scope and moisture readings help describe the work. Your insurer decides coverage, not us.

FAQ

Downtown Bartow water damage questions

Do you take calls from businesses as well as homes in downtown Bartow?

Yes. We take calls for shops, offices and nearby homes. Business jobs are described on commercial water damage restoration.

Does a repair in the historic district need city approval?

It can. The city's Historic Architectural Review Board reviews changes to contributing properties inside designated districts. Emergency drying is different from altering the exterior, so check with the city before any outside change.

A ceiling stain showed up under an upstairs bathroom. Is that urgent?

Treat it as active until proven otherwise. A stain means water has reached the ceiling material. Shut off the fixture if you can, keep people out from under it and call.

What should I say when I call from downtown?

Give the street address and cross street, where the water came from, how deep it is, and whether the power is on. Mention whether you rent the space or own the building.

Is downtown Bartow in a FEMA flood zone?

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Flood zones are set by address, so look yours up on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.
