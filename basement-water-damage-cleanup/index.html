Basement flooding emergency: stay out of deep water

Water in a below-grade room is an emergency, and the 24/7 line is open at any hour for Bartow callers. Same-day service is offered when crews are available. Water that is deeper than the bottom stair, or near outlets, the panel or a water heater, is not safe to enter.

Turn off power to the area only if the panel is dry and outside the wet space, and call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas concern. Do not pump a flooded basement too fast during a storm, because saturated soil outside can push walls inward if the inside is emptied abruptly.

Honest scope: this is not waterproofing

Many Florida homes have no basement, so this page is for the properties that do, such as buildings with a lower level, a sunken room, a walk-out unit or a below-grade garage. Water restoration removes water, cleans and dries. It does not seal a foundation or fix grading.

If water comes back every time it rains, a waterproofing or drainage contractor is the right trade for the cause. We tell callers that up front, because fixing the cause after the cleanup is what prevents a repeat.

What to do before we arrive at a wet basement

  • Stay out if water is deep or reaches outlets, the panel or an appliance.
  • Use the stairs with care, because wet steps are slick.
  • Shut off the source if it is a pipe, and check that any sump pump is running.
  • Do not pump a flooded basement out all at once, since saturated soil outside can press hard on the walls.
  • Call (844) 950-6900 and describe the depth and the source.

Where basement water comes from

Groundwater seepage

Water pushes through floor joints and wall cracks when the soil outside is saturated after heavy rain.

Sump pump failure

A pump that fails or loses power during a storm lets groundwater rise into the room.

Drain and sewer backup

The lowest drain is often a basement floor drain, which can back up during a main blockage. See sewage backup cleanup.

Plumbing and appliance leaks

A burst line or failed water heater floods the room from above. See water heater leak cleanup.

What happens to a finished basement

Carpet, pad, insulation and drywall that touched groundwater or sewage are usually removed, because groundwater is treated as Category 2 or 3 under the S500 standard. Stored boxes, furniture and electronics are sorted into restorable and disposable.

Concrete floors and block walls dry slowly, and drying them takes air movers and dehumidifiers run for days. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours, and a below-grade room is often the hardest place to meet that goal.

Flood versus groundwater, and the paperwork

Rising surface water from a river or lake is a flood, and the cleanup follows flood damage cleanup. FEMA says homeowners and renters policies do not typically cover flood damage, and a National Flood Insurance Program policy generally takes effect 30 days after purchase.

Many policies also treat groundwater seepage and sump failure differently from a burst pipe, so ask your insurer what applies to your situation. The Florida Department of Financial Services says to report claims promptly and mitigate further damage, and to photograph before temporary repairs.

FAQ

Common questions

Do you handle basement water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Bartow?

Yes. The line is open 24/7, and same-day service is offered when crews are available and the area is safe to enter.

Do you waterproof basements?

No. Restoration removes water and dries the space. A waterproofing or drainage contractor addresses the cause.

Can I pump the water out myself?

For a small amount, with safe electrical conditions. For deep water, pump gradually and avoid going in if electricity is involved.

Will my carpet and drywall be saved?

Clean water caught quickly can sometimes be dried in place. Groundwater or sewage usually means removal.

Does insurance cover basement flooding?

Policies vary. Flood is usually excluded from standard homeowners coverage, so check for separate flood coverage and ask your insurer.
