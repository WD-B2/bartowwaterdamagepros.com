Commercial water emergency: protect the building and the business

A commercial water loss is an emergency because the building is a business asset, and the 24/7 line is open for Bartow shops, offices and multi-tenant properties at any hour. Same-day service is offered when crews are available, so call as soon as you find water.

Close the main valve or the valve to the failed line, and if sprinkler water is flowing, call the fire department and the sprinkler contractor. Move inventory, papers and electronics above the waterline, keep staff out of wet areas near panels and outlets, and take photographs of the damage before anything is moved.

How commercial jobs differ from homes

Larger square footage

Open floor plans and long runs of wall mean more extraction, more air movers and more dehumidifiers than a typical house.

Shared walls and floors

In multi-unit and strip buildings, water crosses into neighbors through shared walls, so speed matters.

Downtime pressure

The goal is often to reopen part of the space quickly, which means phased drying and careful scheduling.

Contents and records

Inventory, equipment, documents and electronics need to be triaged before they are damaged further.

What to do before we arrive at your business

  • Shut off the water at the main and tell the building manager.
  • Keep staff and customers out of wet areas and away from outlets.
  • Photograph inventory, equipment and finishes before they are moved.
  • List what was affected, and call your insurer before signing a repair contract.
  • Call (844) 950-6900 so crews can plan equipment and access.

Common commercial sources

  • Failed supply lines and fixtures in restrooms and break rooms
  • Roof leaks over flat or low-slope roofs during summer storms
  • Fire sprinkler discharge or a broken sprinkler head
  • Water heater and HVAC condensate failures in mechanical rooms
  • Sewer or drain backups in restaurants and multi-tenant buildings

Bartow has a downtown commercial district of older frame and classical revival buildings, and older buildings often have mixed or older plumbing and shared walls between storefronts. Water in one storefront can show up next door, so check adjoining spaces and tell the property manager at once.

How the job is organized

  1. 1

    Stabilize

    Stop the source and protect contents and electrical equipment.

  2. 2

    Extract and remove

    Standing water is pumped, and wet carpet, ceiling tile and drywall that cannot dry are removed.

  3. 3

    Dry in phases

    Equipment is placed to dry priority areas first so part of the space can reopen sooner.

  4. 4

    Document

    Photos, moisture logs and a scope are prepared for insurers and property owners.

Sewage and flood water in a commercial building are Category 3 and follow the steps on sewage backup cleanup and flood damage cleanup. Drying methods are explained on the structural drying page.

Tenants, landlords and insurance

In a leased space, the lease decides who owns the building loss and who owns the tenant improvements and contents, so read it and tell the landlord immediately. Business policies vary on property damage and loss of income, and flood is usually excluded from standard coverage.

Ask your insurer in plain terms what it covers, keep receipts, and ask what documentation it needs before cleanup starts. Do not assume a policy covers business interruption without checking the wording. Storefronts and offices in the historic core are covered on Downtown Bartow.

FAQ

Common questions

Do you handle commercial water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Bartow?

Yes. The line is open 24/7, and same-day service is offered when crews are available.

Can you work while the business stays open?

Often partly. Crews can isolate wet areas and dry in phases so part of the space stays usable, depending on the layout and the hazard.

Do you work with property managers and landlords?

Yes. Documentation, photographs and moisture logs are prepared in a form that can be shared with owners and insurers.

Does business insurance cover downtime?

Some policies include loss-of-income coverage, and many require a covered cause. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

Who pays in a leased space?

The lease usually decides, so read it and tell the landlord immediately.
