Storm damage emergency: water coming in during or after a storm

Storm water that enters through the roof, a window or a wall is an emergency, and the 24/7 line takes storm calls at night and on weekends. Same-day service is offered when crews are available, though demand can spike after a severe storm.

Stay away from downed power lines and from any wet area near the panel or outlets. If it is safe, move belongings away from the leak, catch drips and photograph the damage. A roofer or contractor has to close the opening, because water will keep entering as long as it is open.

How storm water differs from other water

This page covers rain and wind that get through the building envelope, such as lifted shingles, a failed vent boot or a broken window. It is not rising ground water, which is a flood and is covered on flood damage cleanup.

Rain that comes through a roof is usually Category 1 at first, but it passes through insulation, dirt and debris, and it can slide to Category 2 as it travels. Treat any storm water that sat for a long time with caution.

What to do during and after a storm

  • Stay away from downed lines and anything touching them.
  • Do not walk or drive through floodwater, since the National Weather Service says six inches can knock over an adult.
  • Place buckets under roof drips and move belongings away from wet walls.
  • Photograph the damage before temporary repairs, then call your insurer.

Bartow storm timing and why it matters

The National Weather Service Tampa Bay office defines the rainy season for West Central Florida as May 25 to October 10, and the Atlantic hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30. Heavy storms, wind-driven rain and tropical systems can hit at any point in those windows.

In October 2024, Hurricane Milton put Lake Hancock at emergency levels, according to Polk County. Wind and rain from a tropical system can open roofs and push water through windows well before any river or lake rises, so damage to the building envelope often arrives first.

What gets wet, and what the crew does

Attics and ceilings

Insulation soaks first and holds water, then the ceiling below stains and sags. See ceiling water damage repair.

Walls and windows

Wind-driven rain gets behind siding and around frames, and wet drywall can sit unseen for days.

Floors

Hardwood and carpet near broken windows absorb water and need quick drying.

Contents

Furniture, electronics and boxes in rooms with leaks need to be moved and checked.

Crews extract standing water, then map moisture with meters and a thermal camera. Wet insulation and drywall that will not dry are removed, and drying equipment runs until readings are normal. The structural drying page explains the equipment.

Documenting storm damage for insurance

Photograph the roof opening, the broken window and the water damage before anything is moved. Keep receipts for tarps, emergency repairs and hotel stays. Many Florida policies treat wind damage differently from other perils, so ask your insurer whether a separate hurricane or named-storm deductible applies.

The Florida Department of Financial Services says initial and reopened claims should be reported within one year of the date of loss, and supplemental claims within 18 months. Report the loss promptly, protect the property from further damage and contact your insurer before signing a repair contract.

FAQ

Common questions

Do you handle storm water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Bartow?

Yes. The line is open 24/7, and same-day service is offered when crews are available. Demand can rise after a major storm.

Does storm damage mean flood damage?

Not necessarily. Rain through a roof or window is storm damage, while rising ground water is a flood. They are usually covered differently by insurance.

Do you tarp the roof?

A roofer or contractor covers roof openings. The restoration crew handles extraction, drying and interior cleanup.

How long do I have to report a storm claim in Florida?

The Florida Department of Financial Services says initial claims should be reported within one year of the date of loss, and supplemental claims within 18 months.

Is it safe to stay in the house?

Not if there is structural damage, an electrical hazard or a ceiling holding water. If in doubt, leave and call the utility or 911.
